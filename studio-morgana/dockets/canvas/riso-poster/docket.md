name: Risograph poster
niche: canvas
kinds: event poster, exhibition poster, festival poster, market poster, magazine cover, zine cover
size: 1080 by 1666 (1:1.54, close to 2:3); for 4:5 (1080 by 1350) keep the headline zone and shorten the image zone
tags: risograph, print, two-colour, spot ink, overprint, grain, editorial, warm, tactile
use when: events, exhibitions, markets, festivals, small publications; anything that should feel printed by hand, warm and local; works with a photo, an uploaded illustration, or only shapes

## Look
Two flat spot inks on cream paper (or one ink on a coloured ground). A tight headline in the top third, small capital
labels in a right-hand column, and an image or big shapes filling the lower two thirds and bleeding off the page.
Where the inks cross they multiply into a third, darker colour. Everything carries a coarse grain, as if printed.

## Grid
Measured on six riso posters (proportions of the width W and height H):
- Margin: 6% of W on the left, right and top (66 at 1080). Labels start 4–6% of H from the top.
- Headline zone: the top 30–35% of H. The headline sits flush left on the margin and takes 55–75% of W, leaving a
  right-hand column of 25–35% for labels.
- Label column: starts at 62–76% of W. Two groups: one at the very top (what it is), one lower, level with the
  headline's last lines (where and when). Some posters add a third group at the bottom left, above the bottom margin.
- Image zone: from 35–40% of H to the bottom edge, bleeding off the bottom and at least one side. Its top reaches
  5–10% of H up into the headline zone (a sun, a leaf tip, a head), so the two zones lock together.
- Variants: a full-width masthead fitted margin to margin (magazine cover); the headline in the bottom third over a
  full-bleed image (film poster); hand-set type turned a few degrees (zine).

## Type
- Headline: a bold text serif (Newsreader bold) for warm and editorial, or a condensed sans (Big Shoulders Display)
  for loud. Sentence case for the serif, capitals for the condensed sans. 2–4 lines, one or two words a line.
- Headline size: the serif at 12–13% of W (137 at 1080), cap height about 9% of W; the condensed sans at 15–23% of W.
- Line step: 0.95–1.0 of the size for the serif (descenders nearly touch the next line), 0.87 for the condensed sans.
- Labels: Jost 500, capitals, 2.7% of W (29 at 1080), tracking 2, line step 1.4 of the size, aligned left within the
  column (a date may align right on the right margin). Groups of two to four lines, one blank line between groups.
- Two fonts only.

## Palette
Paper plus two inks, a warm fluorescent one and a cool deep one, or one ink on a coloured ground:
- Paper #f1e9da; pink #ff4fa3 with blue #2343c2.
- Paper #f1e9da; orange #ff6a1f with blue #2343c2.
- Paper #f1e9da; pink #ff4fa3 with teal #1a9a8f.
- Paper #f1e9da; violet #7b5cd6 with green #3fbf6a.
- Yellow ground #f3c43d; one red ink #df372c (headline, labels and the whole image in the one ink).
Roles: the warm ink takes the headline and one big flat shape; the cool ink takes the image's detail and the labels
(or the other way round, but never mix within a role).

## Image
- A photo becomes two inks by printing it twice: once with "duotone <cool> and <paper>" (the cool ink takes the
  shadows), then again with "duotone #ffffff and <warm>" (the warm ink takes the highlights), both multiply, the second
  9–12 px (about 1% of W) right and down: the misregistration that makes it riso.
- One ink on a coloured ground: "duotone <ink> and <ground>" once, no second pass.
- An illustration that is already in the inks: print it as it is, and set the ground to the illustration's own ground
  colour so no edge shows.
- Without an image: big flat shapes in the two inks (a sun circle, rows of stems and blooms, blocks), multiply.

## Materials
Flat ("Let there be a flat canvas"). The texture is the grain: "Make the card paper, grainy 0.25–0.35, coarse".

## Signature
Overprint. The warm shape sits behind the cool image (or the headline crosses the image) and the overlap multiplies
into a third colour. At least one overlap must be visible.

## Don't
- Use more than two inks (plus paper or a coloured ground), a gradient, a shadow or a glow.
- Centre the headline (except the full-width masthead variant).
- Put the second print of a photo in register: offset it 9–12 px.
- Use a dark photo for the warm pass as it is: the warm ink only prints the highlights, so a dark photo prints almost
  nothing; pick a lighter photo or give the warm ink the shadows instead.
- Write "feathered" on a printed photo: it blurs the whole photo.
- Write "see-through" when you mean opacity: "10% see-through" is 90% opaque.
- Name a thing "where" (it is a word the language uses).

## Slots
Change: the headline (2–4 short lines), the label groups (what, where, when), the ink pair, the image (photo,
illustration or shapes), the image's crop ("focused at x y").
Keep: the margin, the headline zone and label column, the image bleeding off the bottom, two fonts, two inks, the grain.

## How we did it
- Lines 3–7: paper and the two inks as named colours; the card is paper with coarse grain.
- Lines 9–15: the image zone without a photo: a warm sun circle, then cool stems and blooms in rows, all multiply,
  so the blooms crossing the sun turn deep violet.
- Lines 17–22: the headline style (Newsreader bold, 140, flush left, warm), three lines at baselines 190, 330, 470
  (a step equal to the size), multiply.
- Lines 24–32: the label style (Jost 500, 29, tracking 2, cool) and two groups in the right-hand column: x 790 for
  the top group, x 690 for the where-and-when group level with the headline.
